// the pattern

Three companies before twenty. I chase money from real wallets, not applause. And money is not where I stop, it is where I earn the right to build something far bigger.

This is the honest version of that story. Failures stamped, not hidden. Because the failures are the reason I know exactly what I am doing now.

01
deploy 01 — pink parcel Failed

Pink Parcel

My first company sold period-care kits. It did not work.

I started it at 16 with a team of 5: 300+ paid orders across 6 hostels, sold door to door. Hand delivery could not grow past campus. But it put me in the game. It turned a student into a founder, and a founder does not un-become one. Most people never pass the filter of actually starting. I passed it, and I never looked back. Failure one was tuition, not defeat.

At the campus fest I sold one person at a time, while our Mickey walked the crowd in the summer heat with a QR code on his chest.

FemoraAI

An AI that reads a woman's body and explains it back to her. This one did almost everything right.

Jan 2025 to Jul 2026. Each user got her own cycle model built with Kalman filters, not a population average. // what two startups taught me
01

Pivot fast.

Loyalty to an idea that will not pay is not grit. It is ego. I move the moment the money says move.

02

Talk first, build second.

Luna came out of 1,500+ cold messages and 31 founder calls across the US and UK before we wrote any code. Then it shipped in 10 days.

03

The gap was never the tech.

It was distribution. Two founders who both wrote code, and no one who sold. That was the whole problem.

I build it. Then I sell it.

At FemoraAI I owned the React Native frontend and product architecture, wrote Spring Boot microservices and designed our per-user cycle model. Before that I ran a live app on AWS at 99.9% uptime. Now I lead growth and distribution for Luna, because that was the missing layer. I can still build every layer underneath it.
